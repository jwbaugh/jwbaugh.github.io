Computational Modeling Group

Our group develops models of software, to make it more robust and reliable—and models of engineering and scientific systems, to improve the way we design and analyze artifacts in the real world.

Areas we draw from include

formal methods: mathematical techniques for specifying, developing, and verifying software systems

systems science: computational approaches from operations research to help make better decisions

Applications in engineering include coastal hazard mitigation, civil site development, vehicle routing and scheduling, air quality management, structural optimization and design, and active control systems.

Our projects touch on many different facets of problem solving, from theoretical aspects and formal models to the practical issues of building complete systems that incorporate state-of-the-art technology.

Representative Projects

Alloy case studies – a collection of software models that allow us to draw useful conclusions about the structure and behavior of computer programs.

Subdomain modeling – an exact reanalysis technique for storm surge and tides that enables the assessment of local subdomain changes with less computational effort than would be required by a complete resimulation of the full domain. Subdomain modeling is now built into the official ADCIRC release. Funded by the US Department of Homeland Security (DHS).

Vitri – an object-oriented framework implemented in Java for high-performance distributed computing. Its capabilities include an asynchronous global-parallel genetic algorithm that is particularly suited for coarse-grained tasks executing on platforms that have variations in processor speeds. Funded by the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

Dial-a-ride scheduler – a multiobjective vehicle routing and scheduling system using genetic algorithms and simulated annealing, implemented in Java for workstation networks. Funded by the Federal Transit Administration (FTA), NC DOT, and the Winston-Salem Transit Authority (WSTA).

Strategy development tool – an optimization-based decision-support system for environmental regulators evaluated by the NC Department of Environment and Natural Resources (NC DENR) and EPA Region 3. The SDT allows conjunctive use of optimization tools, including genetic algorithms, simulated annealing, multivariate regression, and simple heuristics.
